Embedded

Three harmonic populations, each locked onto its own fundamental by default and mutually unrelated to the other two — three organised, mutually illegible identities. A single control, permeability, governs how strongly they — and a live, silent voice built from microphone pitch-tracking — pull toward a shared consensus. Past its critical value the populations' own fundamentals bend together and the field's stereo image collapses to centre. Unlike the earlier piece, this is not a loop you can simply turn back off: once genuinely embedded, dropping permeability to zero does not un-embed it. Only re-individuate does.

Within-population order — rk

Each population's own internal coherence. This should stay high through the whole permeability range — collapsing into a shared identity is not the same as dissolving into noise. If a bar drops near zero, that population has lost itself, not just its boundary.

One-way door — S against permeability

Past Θ≈1.8 the field locks. Sweep back down and watch the trace not retrace itself — it stays near the top all the way to Θ=0. That is the claim this piece makes instead of v2's ordinary hysteresis loop: embeddedness, once reached, does not need permeability to hold it.

The room — microphone

microphone off — the room carries only its floor weight
Audio from the microphone is analysed locally for its dominant pitch and how clear that pitch is — never played back, never sent anywhere. A confident, sustained tone counts for more in the census than noise or silence does, the same way the source study found a clear, "ethereal" musical context reorganised the brain more than resting or watching a silent film did.

Keys — a context pulse, not a fundamental

A key briefly makes the room's voice unmistakably clear at that pitch — a stand-in for "the room suddenly plays a clear tone." Below critical Θ the field ignores it. Above, the shared identity audibly bends toward it. esc stops.
