Breath

A pacer. Audio suggests a rhythm for your lungs — not for your brainwaves — because pacing a motor behaviour is something sound is reliably good at, and respiration has its own well-evidenced route to autonomic and cortical state. Nothing here listens to you, scores you, or verifies that anything happened. There are no permissions to grant and no number to optimise: your own sense of your body is the only instrument pointed at it. The cue has no ticks and no attacks, so drifting off the pace produces no click and no sense of having failed. Breathe with it or around it.

Layers

Three independent layers, each with its own switch and level — c cue, b breath, d drone. Cue is the harmonic stack whose brightness follows the breath. Breath is pink noise driven by airflow rather than lung volume, so it swells mid-inhale and goes silent through retentions on its own; faster protocols are genuinely louder. Drone is the steady reference tone from the musical system generator — it does not move with the breath at all, which is the point of it. Turn the cue off during Box breathing to hear why it exists: half that cycle is retentions, and breath alone leaves them with no cue.

Drone

A pitch that is an anchor shifted by whole periods, sounding as one voice or as a pair straddling it. The ratio is geometric and the hertz offset arithmetic, applied in that order, so each is exact in its own terms: 3/2 puts precisely a just fifth between the voices, 6 Hz puts precisely 6 Hz.

The spread is worth playing with here specifically: a pair beats at its hertz spread, and breath rates live in the same range. 6 breaths/min is 0.1 Hz, so a spread of 0.1 beats once per breath. v1 built its whole clock this way — two tones 1.5 cents apart beating at 0.5 Hz, every later event timed by it.

Drone voice — A/B

Switches live, mid-breath, without interrupting anything — press v to cycle. All three are level-matched to −20.76 dBFS over a full breath, because a few dB decides an A/B on loudness alone regardless of timbre. blind hides which is which and shuffles them, since you already know which one I argued for.

Listen at the bottom of the breath — that is where they differ. Dense is rough there (all 25 partials sit inside one critical band). Sparse is smooth but its implied pitch is an octave high at the bottom and falls as you inhale, so pitch and brightness move in opposite directions. Balanced is smooth and holds 36 Hz throughout.

Coming down

Decelerates from wherever it is into slow breathing, and never cuts to silence. Deliberately a large target and bound to the two easiest keys: retentions can bring on tetany, hands cramp, and fine motor control is the first thing to go — so this cannot be a small button.

Before you use this

Never do paced or heavy breathing in or near water — not a pool, a bath, or the sea. Low CO₂ removes the urge to breathe while oxygen keeps falling, so you can lose consciousness with no warning. Practise lying down or seated where a faint cannot injure you. Tingling or cramping hands are common and pass as CO₂ returns. Skip this entirely if you are pregnant or have cardiovascular disease, a seizure history, glaucoma, an aneurysm, uncontrolled hypertension, panic disorder, or a history of psychosis or bipolar. For anything longer or heavier than slow paced breathing, have somebody else present — that is what the traditions do, and no feature here replaces it.
